01 — THE PROBLEM

The old app worked. Nobody had a reason to come back.

I read the existing codebase before drawing anything. It had the content — devotionals, audio sermons, articles, announcements — and no shape. Tabs were nouns from the database. The home screen was a devotional and nothing else. And two things were quietly broken in ways the team hadn't noticed.
The Live tab was never live
It built a eventType=live request, never sent it, then displayed the newest upload from the channel and labelled it LIVE. On a Tuesday afternoon the app told you a service was streaming.
The first tap on a sermon did nothing
The audio context read stale React state inside its own play handler instead of the track passed in, and its cleanup unloaded the sound on every change — racing whatever had just been created.
Reading the code first is the part of this project I'd defend hardest. Neither bug is visible in a screenshot, and both are the kind of thing that quietly teaches people the app doesn't work.
02 — THE SYSTEM

One accent, used once per screen.

The brand is teal and turquoise. The first pass used the whole category palette from the codebase — sky, amber, coral — and every screen turned into a fruit salad where nothing led. So I cut it to a rule strict enough to hold across seventeen screens.
Teal #41BBAC
The only accent. Marks the one thing to touch, and progress fills. Type on light drops to #1C6462 for contrast.
Magenta #CB3CA0
Appears exactly twice in the entire app: the verse card on Home, the verse block in the reader. Nowhere else.
Ink #12100F
Chrome, nav pill, secondary actions. Does the structural work colour usually gets asked to do.
Paper #F3F1EC / #FFF
Warm ground, white cards. Carries 90% of every screen so the teal has somewhere to land.
THE ONE GLOW
You are the light of the world.
Glow is a single effect used on a single word — the theme word of the church. Not a glassmorphic surface, not a hover state, not a card border. Once, on purpose, and it means something.
TYPE
Plus Jakarta Sans
at 800, tight.
Instrument Serif for scripture only.
Two families, one job each. The serif never appears outside a verse, so scripture reads as a different kind of voice without a single decorative flourish.
03 — THE SCREENS

Built around a week, not a database.

Five tabs, named for what a member does: Home, Watch, Listen, Give, News. Sermons are a mix of shorts and full messages because that's how the church already publishes — so the app has a vertical shorts player and a chaptered message player, not one generic video list.
Home
Verse, shorts rail, live tile, project pool. Everything you'd open the app for, above one scroll.
Shorts
Full-bleed vertical, action rail on the right. The format young members already understand.
Give
Tithe, seed, or a project pool with real progress. Card, transfer, USSD, wallet — and guests can give.
THE DECISION I'D DEFEND
Signing up is optional. All of it.
Sermons, live, announcements, the devotional — and giving — all work with no account. An account buys one thing: sync. The onboarding says that in plain words instead of dark-patterning people into a form.
Guest actions write to local storage under guest:*, and signing in later hands them over. A first-time visitor should never meet a wall before they've met the church.
Now playing
Resumes where you stopped, plays in the background, downloads for the drive to church.
CSGs
CSGs meet in person, so the app doesn't try to host them — it carries their admin's updates to the right eighteen people.
Reader
Long-form set for actual reading, with a share card so a verse can leave the app.
04 — THE CRAFT

Every phone, no device branching.

This congregation is on everything from a 360dp Galaxy A-series to a Pixel 9 Pro XL to an iPhone 15. There is not one Platform.OS === layout branch in the app. Three mechanisms cover the range.
×0.86–1.3
A clamped scale layer
Every size passes through one function against a 390×844 baseline, clamped at both ends. Small phones never cramp; big ones never go cartoonish. Type scales at half the rate of space.
insets
Real safe areas, one path
Notch, Dynamic Island, Samsung punch-hole, Android 3-button nav, gesture bar — all resolved from the same inset call. Nothing hard-coded, nothing guessed.
520dp
Then it stops stretching
Above that the column centres, so a tablet, an unfolded Z Fold or split-screen gets a real layout instead of 2000px text lines. Unfolding re-renders live.
44pt / 48dp minimum touch targets
OS font-scale respected, capped at 125%
Android 15 edge-to-edge
Keyboard pan, not resize
Offline-first fallbacks on every fetch
05 — SHIPPED

Handed over as running code, plus an honest list of what isn't built.

The app reads the church's existing Firestore and Storage, so content published from the admin panel appears with no migration. Where the design needs a backend that doesn't exist — payments, CSGs, push — I wrote the endpoint spec rather than faking it and letting someone discover the gap in sprint three.
Wired and working
Devotionals · verse of the day · announcements · audio sermons · GOAKS · full messages · short clips · articles · auth · real live detection · uploads with duration-based shorts/full split
Specced, not built
Paystack initialize/verify/webhook · saved cards · virtual accounts · USSD · recurring gifts · project pools · CSGs and CSG-scoped push · device tokens · saves/notes/progress sync · live chat · account deletion
Eleven sections, every endpoint named with its request shape and the reason it matters. The engineer picking this up knows exactly what Monday looks like.
WHAT I TOOK FROM IT
The restraint was the work. Three rounds of this design were louder, and all three were worse.
I started with the full category palette because the codebase had it. It looked busy and led nowhere. Cutting to one accent, one glow and two magenta surfaces is what made the app feel calm enough to open every morning — which was the entire brief.
